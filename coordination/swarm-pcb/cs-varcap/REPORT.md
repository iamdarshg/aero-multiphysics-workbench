# Code-SDR — varactor-capacitor tuning over 0.1–10 GHz

Scope: can a varactor tune the Code-SDR front end across its whole operating
regime (0.1–10 GHz), and if not, where can it honestly go?

All numbers below are produced by `scripts/cs_varcap.py` (run log:
`cs_varcap_run.log`, tables: `cs_varcap_*.csv`). No datasheet value is
transcribed; part-family ratios are the ones supplied in the task brief.

## 0. The architectural fact that decides everything

The frequency plan tunes with **fractional-N synthesizers, not with reactances**.
`FREQUENCY_PLAN.csv` fixes `first_if_mhz = 2426.25`, `second_lo_mhz = 2400` and
`second_if_mhz = 26.25` on **all seven rows**; only LO1 moves
(`first_lo_min/max_mhz`). LO1 is generated by U40 (LMX2592, `HARDWARE.md:67`)
and LO2 by U41 (ADF4360-1). Consequences:

- Channel selection is already solved by the PLL. A varactor is not needed for
  tuning and cannot improve on it in Q, phase noise or repeatability.
- The second-mixer image is at a **fixed** frequency (2353.75–2393.75 MHz for
  wanted IF1 2406.25–2446.25 MHz, `RF_ANALYSIS.md:124-127`), so image rejection
  is a fixed-frequency job done by FL70 (QPQ1909 BAW). It does not move with
  the RF band, therefore it needs **no tuning element at all**.
- The only remaining roles for a varactor are trims of fixed nodes and,
  optionally, an antenna-side tracking preselector that the plan does not
  currently have (`rf_preselector` = fixed 2.5 GHz LPF on LOW, "band-matched
  LNA plus optional external filter" on the high rows).

## 1. Model and constants (as specified)

    C(V) = C0 / (1 + V/Vj)^M ,   M = 0.5 ,  Vj = 0.7 V

and for a fixed inductor `f = 1/(2*pi*sqrt(L*C))`, so

    C0/Cmin required = (f_hi/f_lo)^2 ,   V needed = Vj*(R^2 - 1)

| V (V) | C/C0 | f/f0 | (1/C)dC/dV (/V) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | −0.7143 |
| 0.35 | 0.8165 | 1.1067 | −0.4762 |
| 0.70 | 0.7071 | 1.1892 | −0.3571 |
| 1.40 | 0.5774 | 1.3161 | −0.2381 |
| 3.30 | 0.4183 | 1.5461 | −0.1250 |
| 5.00 | 0.3504 | 1.6893 | −0.0877 |
| 15.00 | 0.2112 | 2.1762 | −0.0318 |

**Hard ceiling of this model** (this is the single most useful number in the
report): with M = 0.5 and Vj = 0.7 V the achievable capacitance ratio is
`(1 + V/Vj)^0.5`, so

| control 0…Vcc | max C0/Cmin | max f_hi/f_lo |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3 V (RP2040/DAC rail) | 2.390 : 1 | 1.546 : 1 |
| 5.0 V (`VIN_5V` exists) | 2.854 : 1 | 1.689 : 1 |
| 12 V (needs a boost) | 4.259 : 1 | 2.064 : 1 |
| 15 V (typ. breakdown) | 4.736 : 1 | 2.176 : 1 |
| 30 V (needs a boost) | 6.622 : 1 | 2.573 : 1 |

So **no bias network reaches 10:1 with M = 0.5**: a quoted 10:1 at ≤15 V implies
M substantially greater than 0.5 on that part. Treat the family "3:1…10:1"
ratings as achievable only with the real (higher-M) exponent, not with the
exponent fixed by this assignment — see UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Real parts against those ratios

Family capability as given in the brief (no datasheet was read here):

| family | ratio | useful to |
|---|---|---|
| Skyworks SMV1231–1245 | ~3:1 … 10:1 | ~2 GHz |
| Skyworks SMV1405–1430 | ~3:1 … 6:1 | ~3–6 GHz |
| Macom MA46H | "microwave" (ratio UNVERIFIED) | microwave |

- P1/P1a/P1b (100:1), P2_LOW (625:1), P2_LOW_HF (62 500:1), P4band (54.8:1):
  **no part, at any voltage, ever.** A 10:1 frequency span needs a 100:1
  capacitance span; the hyperbolic model tops out at 4.74:1 even at 15 V.
- P5_LOW / P5_HIGH (4.09 / 4.28:1): only reachable at ≥12–15 V control, i.e. a
  new boost rail on a board whose only input is 5 V (`HARDWARE.md` §9), and the
  high LO port spans to 9.78 GHz — **outside both SMV families**, so only MA46H
  could even be considered.
- P2_HIGH_A (3.335:1) needs 7.08 V → not reachable from 3.3 V or 5 V.
- P2_HIGH_B (2.621:1) needs 4.11 V → 5 V rail is sufficient, 3.3 V is not.
- P2_HC_HS / P2_HC_LS / P2_HIGH_D (1.17 / 1.66 / 1.56:1) fit in 3.3 V.
- P3, P4, P4trim, P4_aa (≤1.44:1): trivially inside any part, including at
  VHF where SMV1231–1245 is at home.

### 3b. The 6–10 GHz construction limit

A lumped resonator needs `L = 1/((2*pi*f)^2 * C)`:

| f | C | L needed | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 GHz | 1 pF | 0.396 nH | below a via (~0.3 nH) |
| 8 GHz | 2 pF | 0.198 nH | parasitic-dominated |
| 10 GHz | 1 pF | 0.253 nH | parasitic-dominated |
| 10 GHz | 2 pF | 0.127 nH | parasitic-dominated |
| 10 GHz | 5 pF | 0.051 nH | not constructible |
| 2.4 GHz | 3.3 pF | 1.333 nH | fine |
| 26.25 MHz | 1000 pF | 36.76 nH | fine |

So on FR4, **above ~6 GHz there is no varactor preselector to build**: the
"inductor" would have to be smaller than the via that connects it. This kills
P2_HC_LS and P2_HIGH_D even though their ratios fit in 3.3 V, and it is the
real answer for 10 GHz.

## 4. Linearity

Monotonicity: `C(V)` is strictly decreasing and single-valued on 0…Vbd, so
tuning is monotonic with no direction hysteresis **up to the control ceiling**;
if the bias network can transiently drive the junction forward (control below
ground, or RF swing taking the anode positive) the junction conducts and the
capacitance *stops* moving — that is the practical monotonicity failure, not the
model.

Steepness (this is the linearity problem in numbers):

- Peak fractional sensitivity `|dlnC/dV| = M/(Vj+V) = 0.7143 /V` at V = 0
  (71.4 % per volt), falling to 0.0318 /V at 15 V — a **22.5:1 slope change**
  across the control range.
- Slope falls to **half** its peak at exactly V = Vj = 0.700 V. The first 0.7 V
  consumes 29.3 % of C0 and delivers an 18.9 % frequency pull — huge authority
  in the region where the law is most nonlinear.
- Fraction of each row's RF span that sits in that steep V < 0.7 V region:

| row | C ratio need | V_max need | steep share (V<0.7 V) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOW | 625 | 273 437 V | 0.8 % |
| LOW_HF_OPTION | 62 500 | 2.7e9 V | 0.1 % |
| HIGH_A | 3.335 | 7.084 V | 22.9 % |
| HIGH_B | 2.621 | 4.110 V | 30.6 % |
| HIGH_C_HIGH_SIDE | 1.168 | 0.255 V | 100 % |
| HIGH_C_LOW_SIDE | 1.656 | 1.219 V | 66.0 % |
| HIGH_D | 1.562 | 1.009 V | 75.7 % |

HIGH_C_HIGH_SIDE is the pathological case: its entire span lives within
0.255 V, i.e. 100 % of the row sits in the steep part, so it cannot be resolved
by a 3.3 V DAC at all.

- Control resolution: 1 LSB of a 12-bit 3.3 V DAC (0.806 mV) at the band-bottom
  local slope `df/dV = f_lo*M/(2*Vj)` gives a **step of 661.8 kHz at 2.3 GHz,
  1.21 MHz at 4.2 GHz, 1.96 MHz at 6.8 GHz, 2.06 MHz at 7.15 GHz and 2.30 MHz
  at 8 GHz**. In the steep region the DAC LSB alone is coarser than the
  channel. Any real design must bias into the flat region (≥ 3–4 V) and
  *trim* there, not tune.
- Large-signal (quasi-static, DC model only): fractional capacitance modulation
  by an in-band tone, 50 Ω reference —

| Pin | Vpk | ΔC/C at 0 V bias | ΔC/C at 2 V bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| −30 dBm | 10.0 mV | 0.71 % | 0.18 % |
| −20 dBm | 31.6 mV | 2.19 % | 0.58 % |
| −10 dBm | 100 mV | 6.46 % | 1.80 % |
| 0 dBm | 316 mV | 17.0 % | 5.39 % |

  Percent-level C modulation is third-order distortion and AM→PM. A varactor in
  the RF path must be biased well away from 0 V, and at mixer LO powers
  (0 dBm and above) it is not usable in the LO path at all. This table is a
  DC-quasi-static bound, not an IMD3 number — no IMD solver was run.

## 5. Where it legitimately goes, and the bill

### P4_aa — IF2 anti-alias / ADC match trim — **ACCEPT (the only clean place)**

The IF2 node is where a varactor is architecturally honest: fixed 26.25 MHz,
and the only thing that ever needs adjusting is the anti-alias corner, which
must sit in the 46.25–50 MHz guard window between the top of the 40 MHz slice
and Nyquist. C ratio 1.169:1, 0.256 V of span.

- Part: **SMV1231–1245** family (3:1…10:1, useful to ~2 GHz — 6.25–50 MHz is
  four decades inside its rating).
- Bias: 0–3.3 V from the RP2040 DAC (or filtered PWM), **operating point at
  2.0–3.0 V**, trim superposition ±0.26 V. 10 kΩ series isolation into the
  control node, 100 nF decoupling, 10 µH RF choke (1.65 kΩ at 26 MHz) so the
  bias network does not load the ladder, 10 nF AC block to the L/C ladder
  (L80–L98 / C201–C294, `HARDWARE.md:113`).
- Placement: on the **LT5560-side** end of the IF2 ladder (U73 output, item
  `HARDWARE.md:108-113`), not at the AD8351/ADC end, because the ADC-end swing
  is what the modulation table above punishes.
- Coverage: **all 7 rows**, because the IF chain is shared after U70/U73 — one
  part serves every band row identically. This is the only sense in which a
  varactor "covers the whole regime": it does not tune it, it trims the one
  shared node the whole regime passes through.

### P3 — BAW coupling match trims at IF1 — REJECT

Needs 1.034:1 (48 mV). The 2390–2393.75 MHz transition uncertainty
(`RF_ANALYSIS.md:126-134`) is a Qorvo measurement-guarantee gap on an *acoustic*
passband; a varactor in the coupling network moves the match, not the acoustic
resonance, so it cannot buy that rejection. 1.2 dB budget loss is a planning
value — fix it with measured S-parameters, not with a trim cap.

### P5 — LO injection match — REJECT

4.089:1 (11.0 V) and 4.283:1 (12.14 V) need a boost rail, the high port reaches
9.78 GHz (outside both SMV families), and the modulation table at LO drive
levels makes a varactor there a phase-noise and AM-PM generator. The LTC5548
LO port is balun-matched; nothing to trim.

### P2 — antenna-side tracking preselector — PARTIAL, and mostly unbuildable

|---|---|---|---|
| LOW | 625 | 273 437 V | impossible |
| LOW_HF_OPTION | 62 500 | 2.7e9 V | impossible |
| HIGH_A | 3.335 | 7.084 V | impossible on 3.3/5 V; needs boost — reject |
| HIGH_B | 2.621 | 4.110 V | ratio OK on `VIN_5V`; 4.2–6.8 GHz at the SMV1405 limit — marginal |
| HIGH_C_HIGH_SIDE | 1.168 | 0.255 V | ratio OK, but 100 % of span in the steep part — unusable |
| HIGH_C_LOW_SIDE | 1.656 | 1.219 V | ratio OK; L = 0.2–0.4 nH → not constructible |
| HIGH_D | 1.562 | 1.009 V | ratio OK; L < via → not constructible |

## 7. Honest limit

- **Nothing in 0.1–2.5 GHz** can be varactor-tuned as a tracking element: the
  LOW row needs 625:1 and LOW_HF needs 62 500:1 against a physical maximum of
  ~4.7:1. Sub-2.5 GHz coverage from a varactor is not a hard design, it is
  arithmetic.
- **6–10 GHz needs something a varactor cannot do**: the ratio is available
  (1.17–1.66:1) but the resonator inductance is 0.05–0.40 nH, i.e. smaller than
  a PCB via, so no lumped varactor preselector exists there. 10 GHz wants a
  switch-filter bank, a switched capacitor bank on SOI, a YIG/BST tuned filter,
  or nothing at all (rely on the BAW at the fixed IF1).
- The only defensible varactor in this design is the **IF2 anti-alias trim**
  (1 part, 1.17:1, 0–3.3 V, all seven rows). Claiming a varactor "tunes
  0.1–10 GHz" is false for this architecture and for these parts.

## 9. UNVERIFIED

- Absolute capacitances (C0, Cmin), breakdown voltage and the real exponent M
  per part: no datasheet was read and `web_search` is unavailable in this
  session (provider ambiguity error). The family ratios used are the brief's.
- The M = 0.5 / Vj = 0.7 model is the assignment's, not a fit; SMV1231–1245's
  quoted 3:1–10:1 cannot be reproduced with M = 0.5 below ~69 V, so the real M
  must be higher. Any ratio-vs-voltage conclusion here is model-bound.
- No S-parameters, no measured C(V), no IMD/IM3 solve, no filter synthesis.
  The modulation table is a DC quasi-static bound, not distortion data.
- Q factor, self-resonance, package parasitics and temperature drift of any
  candidate part are unexamined.
- The IF2 anti-alias corner placement assumes the ladder's present corner is
  near 46.25–50 MHz; the actual ladder response is not in the repo and was not
  simulated.
